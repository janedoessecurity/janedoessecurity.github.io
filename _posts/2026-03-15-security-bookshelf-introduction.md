I'm starting a new series on the blog: the **Security Bookshelf**. The idea is simple — read one cybersecurity-themed book a month and write about it here.

<!--more-->

## The challenge

One book a month. Twelve books a year. Some will be technical, others will cover the human side of security — policy, history, psychology, risk. The only rule is that it has to connect to cybersecurity in some meaningful way.

## Why?

Certifications are great for structured learning, but books offer something different. They give you the space to explore ideas in depth, hear from practitioners who've been in the trenches, and build the kind of broad perspective that makes you better at the job.

I also find that writing about what I've read forces me to actually think about it rather than letting the ideas drift past. If I can explain what a book taught me, I've probably learned something.

## What to expect

Each month I'll post a write-up covering:

- **What the book is about** — a brief, no-spoiler overview
- **Key takeaways** — the ideas that stuck with me
- **Who should read it** — whether it's aimed at beginners, practitioners, or leaders
- **My honest take** — what worked, what didn't, and whether I'd recommend it
