Why I'm Studying for CCSP (And Why That AWS Book Is Now a Very Expensive Paperweight!)
I've decided to pursue the ISC2 CCSP—Certified Cloud Security Professional as my next certification. Having recently passed my CISSP (more on that journey in a future post), sticking with ISC2 made sense. Same award body, different focus - Cloud security felt like the natural next step.

At first, I considered going the vendor-specific route: AWS, GCP, Azure — the usual suspects. I even bought the AWS Solutions Architect study guide, which set me back 30-something quid and is now gathering dust behind my other abandoned certifications books (hi CEH). But ultimately, I decided to stay vendor-neutral and go with CCSP instead.

Here's why:
On-the-job learning...I can pick up vendor-specific tools and consoles as I use them in real environments. There's no substitute for hands-on the job experience.

Vendor certs have a short shelf life. They expire quickly and can become irrelevant between roles. CCSP gives me foundational knowledge that applies across platforms and stays relevant regardless of which cloud provider I'm working with next month or next year.

I'm keeping my options open. I'm increasingly interested in GRC (Governance, Risk, and Compliance), and I'm not ruling out a pivot in that direction. CCSP sits nicely at the intersection of technical security and governance—useful whether I stay hands-on or shift toward a more strategic role.

So that's the plan now: CCSP.
